Your password was never read, stored, or sent anywhere — this is a training page. But a real phishing site captures it the instant you hit "Sign in," alongside everything in the log above.

What should have tipped you off
  • 01
    The web addressReal Microsoft sign-in lives on login.microsoftonline.com. Read the domain before typing a password — not the logo, not the page, the domain.
  • 02
    How you got hereAn unexpected message asking you to "verify," "re-authenticate," or "review a document" is the classic delivery. Urgency is the bait.
  • 03
    You weren't expecting itA login prompt appearing out of nowhere deserves suspicion — even a pixel-perfect one.
  • 04
    The autofill gapYour password manager won't fill credentials on a spoofed domain. That silence is a warning.

What to do instead

Don't click login links in messages. Go to services yourself — type the address or use a bookmark. When in doubt, report the message to IT and delete it. You'll never be in trouble for asking.

Note for the curious: a web page cannot read your search history or the other sites you've visited — the browser deliberately blocks that. Everything above is only what the current visit exposes, which is already far more than most people expect. That's the point.
