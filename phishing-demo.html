Your password was never read, stored, or sent anywhere — this is a training page. But a real phishing site would have captured it the instant you hit “Sign in,” along with everything in the log above.

What should have tipped you off
  • 01
    The web addressReal Microsoft sign-in lives on login.microsoftonline.com. Always read the domain in the address bar before typing a password — attackers use lookalikes like login-microsoft.secure-verify.com.
  • 02
    How you got hereA link in an unexpected email, text, or chat asking you to “verify,” “re-authenticate,” or “review a document” is the classic delivery method. Urgency is the bait.
  • 03
    You weren't expecting itIf you didn't just try to sign in yourself, a login prompt appearing out of nowhere deserves suspicion — even if it looks perfect.
  • 04
    Trust the passkey / autofill gapYour password manager won't offer to fill credentials on a spoofed domain. If autofill doesn't recognize the site, that silence is a warning.

What to do instead

Don't click login links in messages. Navigate to services yourself — type the address or use a bookmark. When in doubt, forward the message to IT and delete it. Reporting a suspicious email is always the right call, and you'll never be in trouble for asking.
